# Community Experience with Automated / Mass-Produced AI Faceless Short Videos (2025-2026)

> Research note, compiled 2026-11-05. **Method limits:** most first-hand hosts were blocked in this session (reddit.com, old.reddit.com, a Reddit mirror at reddit.sentinel-team.org, news.ycombinator.com, community.n8n.io, community.make.com, indiehackers.com, medium.com, substack.com, dexerto.com, tubefilter.com, techcrunch, theverge, 404media, businessinsider, kapwing.com, support.google.com, web.archive.org). WebSearch would not index reddit.com at all ("domain not accessible to our user agent"). So the evidence comes from (a) search-engine summaries of press and blog pages, and (b) **GitHub issues** (search API plus github.com page fetches). GitHub turned out to be the richest source of *first-hand builder* reports: operators of automated Shorts pipelines log quotas, API rejections, policy reviews and view counts there. Each item is tagged **[DATA]** (measured or verified numbers), **[ANECDOTE]** (one person's account), **[PRESS]** (journalism), **[VENDOR/MARKETING]** (sellers of tools or courses, so discount it) or **[OFFICIAL]** (platform text).

## 1. Results: documented experiments, views, RPM, monetization approval or denial

### Takeaway
Verified big wins exist, but they are outliers, and they come from long-form or high-craft AI channels rather than templated Shorts. Fortune verified about $40-60k a month across ~5 channels for one operator, most of it from a six-hour long-form history channel. The few honest small-scale reports of templated AI Shorts show near-zero traction: single-digit to low-hundreds views per video, a few hundred subscribers after 90 days, and Shorts RPM of about $0.02-0.30. Plan for Shorts ad revenue to be negligible. Treat monetization as uncertain under the 2025-2026 "inauthentic content" rules.

### Cited Findings
**Verified or press-documented successes (outliers)**

Fortune checked analytics screenshots and AdSense payout records, which put gross revenue at roughly $700k a year. The channels include "Boring History", a ~6-hour AI-narrated history channel. The pipeline uses Claude for scripts and ElevenLabs for narration, and he spends about 2 hours a day overseeing it. Secondary posts add ~$6,500 a month operating cost, 85-89% margin and ~$60 per video; Fortune is the primary source for the core figures. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/12/30/ai-slop-faceless-youtube-accounts-adavia-davis-user-generated-content/); secondary: [KNOX on X](https://x.com/knoxtwts/status/2023808679137443976). One guide calls Davis's figure "the verified ceiling, not the median" — [tugan.ai](https://tugan.ai/blog/how-to-start-a-faceless-youtube-channel-with-ai). **Note:** this is mostly *long-form* (multi-hour) content, not Shorts.
- [PRESS/ANECDOTE] FUNTASTIC YT (Mark Lawrence Garilao, 21, Philippines) says he earned $9,000 in one month from AI videos: a cartoon kitten and his father in nonsensical scenarios, 1-2 uploads a day, 1-2 hours per video using ChatGPT, KlingAI and an editor. ### Inferences
- A realistic baseline for a new, templated, fully automated Shorts channel is tens to low hundreds of views per video, with high variance and occasional 1k+ outliers. Even at 1M Shorts views a month, $0.03-0.10 RPM means ~$30-100. Shorts ad revenue should not be the project's success metric. Better metrics: views/video, retention, subscribers per 1k views, and traffic to an owned offer or affiliate.
- The verified money-makers (Davis, FUNTASTIC) share three traits: a distinct recurring concept or character, a human spending 1-2 hours a day or per video, and, for Davis, long-form watch time. That points toward a "human-directed batch" model rather than "unattended firehose".

### Gaps
- No independent, aggregate data on what share of AI faceless channels get YPP, or how long it takes. Reddit threads (r/NewTubers, r/PartneredYoutube) could not be read; one search summary mentions spring-2026 r/PartneredYoutube posts saying "inauthentic content" flags are "very hard to appeal", and one creator with 142M total views had demonetization upheld on human review (via a [reddit mirror listing](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/subreddits/PartneredYoutube/snapshots/2026-05-16T15%3A22%3A36.350885Z), unverified).
- No first-hand TikTok Creator Rewards or Facebook Reels payout numbers for AI pages in 2025-2026 were found.
- I could not verify a reported May 2026 case of a 246k-subscriber Reddit-story channel being demonetized; it appears only in the kaigg96 GitHub review.

## 2. Enforcement: demonetization, terminations, bans and platform crackdowns

### Takeaway
Enforcement escalated in four steps:
1. **July 15, 2025:** YouTube YPP renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content", targeting mass-produced, templated videos and judging the whole channel.
2. **Dec 2025-Jan 2026:** headline terminations of 16 AI slop channels (~35M subscribers, ~4.7B views).
3. **~June 2026:** "Automated or synthetic mass-production" was added to YouTube's **Spam policy**, a Community Guidelines rule. Penalties now include strikes and termination, not only demonetization.
4. **Sept 2026:** 20 AI-scripted political channels terminated for spam. **Oct 29, 2026:** a 30-day "fix or be terminated" lock for some spam violations.

Meta (July 2025) and TikTok (Nov 2025) moved in parallel with reduced reach, demonetization, AI sliders and watermarking. Official API pathways (TikTok audit, Instagram publish caps) are themselves a choke point.

### Inferences
- Since ~June 2026 a fully automated channel that uses the *same music, the same visual style and an AI script read aloud* matches YouTube's own spam example almost word for word. The risk is no longer only "no YPP"; it is **strikes and termination**. The Oct 2026 30-day lock suggests YouTube expects many channels in this bucket.
- On TikTok, the project cannot legitimately auto-publish publicly to its *own* accounts through a self-built app. Realistic routes are: an audited third-party SaaS (with shared-quota risk), draft upload plus a manual finish, or building a genuinely public product that passes audit. Browser automation is a ToS and ban risk.
- Meta's penalty applies at account level ("reduced reach for everything"), so cross-posting the same render to many Pages is the highest-risk pattern.

### Gaps
- No primary YouTube changelog date for the spam-policy "mass-production" addition. Kaigg96's mentions of a "May 2026 246k-sub Reddit-story demonetization" could not be confirmed.
- No hard numbers on TikTok shadowbans or account bans from browser-automation uploaders (only vendor claims). No first-hand IP-ban reports for bulk posting were found.

## 3. Practice: cadence, niches, human-in-the-loop, format variation, cost per video, what broke at scale

### Takeaway
Builders who think about policy throttle cadence: 1-2 videos a day, or 1 a week for long-form, deliberately. They vary music, visuals and format per video and keep a human curator or approval step. Reported API cost per Short is about $0.30-1.30 (vendor claims). High-craft AI channels spend ~$60 a video plus 1-2 human hours. At scale things break in the plumbing: YouTube's 10,000-unit daily quota allows ~6 uploads a day, image-API 429s, TikTok audit and user caps, Instagram publish caps, silent upload failures, and slots that are skipped while reviewers go stale.

### Cited Findings
- [DATA, GitHub] YouTube Data API: `videos.insert` costs 1,600 units, and the default quota is 10,000 a day, so about **6 uploads a day per project**. — [75asa/dji2yt #3](https://github.com/75asa/dji2yt/issues/3)
- [DATA, GitHub] On an *unverified* channel, YouTube accepts uploads over 15 minutes but rejects them during processing (`uploadLimitExceeded`). Pipelines must poll `videos.list?part=status` and phone-verify the channel first. — [kick-to-youtube-lives #4](https://github.com/manfromtunis/kick-to-youtube-lives/issues/4)
- [DATA, GitHub] MoneyPrinterV2 (June 2026): image generation with `gemini-3.1-flash-image-preview` returns `429 Too Many Requests` for almost every image when a script has many image prompts. With no retry or backoff, 0 images download and the whole render breaks. Other issues report a bare `except` in `upload_video()` that swallows errors, browser leaks, and an infinite "title too long" loop. — [#266](https://github.com/FujiwaraChoki/MoneyPrinterV2/issues/266), [#225](https://github.com/FujiwaraChoki/MoneyPrinterV2/issues/225), [#265](https://github.com/FujiwaraChoki/MoneyPrinterV2/issues/265)
- [DATA, GitHub] A production pipeline added a "credential probe" on every run (1 quota unit) that names which channel the token authorizes, so wrong-account and wrong-secret failures are never confused. Failed runs are reported as "nothing published, re-run is safe". — [johnAzani/docoga #183](https://github.com/johnAzani/docoga/issues/183)
- [DATA, GitHub] Double-upload risk: if the process crashes after the upload succeeds but before the video ID is stored, the same video is uploaded again and burns 1,600 units. Fix: store a pending-upload token first and embed it in the description. — [dji2yt #3](https://github.com/75asa/dji2yt/issues/3)
- [DATA, GitHub] Throughput decay: Daily Shorts went from 6/6 a day (May 2026) to 8-11 of 15 slots (Nov 2026), and its reviewer agent had been silent for 56+ days. — [Shorts-pipeline #1](https://github.com/caleblschulte0-ux/Shorts-pipeline/issues/1)
- [ANECDOTE, GitHub] Policy-driven cadence: a Reddit-narration channel kept 2 uploads a day and refused 4 a day until it added more music tracks and varied the format. A Japanese AI-BGM project planned 1-2 uploads a week, human final selection with "curation evidence", no single static image looped, and chapters per video. A localization project chose "~1 video/day drip" explicitly to avoid reused/inauthentic flags. — [kaigg96 #80](https://github.com/kaigg96/reddit-daily/issues/80); [hashimoto-katsuaki-eng/ai-agent #239](https://github.com/hashimoto-katsuaki-eng/ai-agent/issues/239); [JovieInc/Jovie #11317](https://github.com/JovieInc/Jovie/issues/11317)
- [ANECDOTE, GitHub] Builders are adding pre-publish "inauthentic content" gates that score template similarity, AI narration over loosely related stock footage, and variation between uploads. One describes the July 2025 rule as an "existential" risk for a not-yet-monetized owner. — [content-generation-platform #86](https://github.com/alessiopagliarulo/content-generation-platform/issues/86); [M17z2025/ai-command-center #106](https://github.com/M17z2025/ai-command-center/issues/106); [Prepublish MCP](https://github.com/cline/mcp-marketplace/issues/2588)
- [ANECDOTE, GitHub] Several 2026 pipelines keep YouTube auto-upload but post to TikTok through Buffer "share-now", or have a human schedule uploads in YouTube Studio from a checklist. That is human-in-the-loop at the publish step. — [mattpocock/course-video-manager #2612](https://github.com/mattpocock/course-video-manager/issues/2612); [HarshJaiswal497/content-engine #12](https://github.com/HarshJaiswal497/content-engine/issues/12)
- [VENDOR] n8n/Make faceless-Shorts template sellers quote API costs of ~$0.30 (Sora 2 via KeyAI), ~$0.60, ~$0.72 (GPT-4.1-mini + Flux + Creatomate + Blotato), ~$0.80 (n8n.io template) and $1.00-1.30 per video. Subscriptions (Blotato, Creatomate, ElevenLabs) and hosting are excluded. ### Inferences
- Plan throughput per platform account rather than "N videos a day overall": YouTube ~6 uploads per API project per day by default; Instagram has a per-account 24-hour publish cap; TikTok depends on audit status and app user caps. Policy-minded operators run well *below* these technical limits (1-2 a day).
- The anti-"mass-produced" levers people actually use: rotate music from a pool, vary visual sources and formats, retire formats that fail, keep human curation evidence, avoid one voice/one template across hundreds of uploads, and add an editorial angle per video.
- Reliability engineering (backoff on 429s, idempotent uploads, upload-status polling, credential probes, alerting on skipped slots) matters as much as content generation.

## 4. Builder opinions on tools (MoneyPrinterTurbo, ShortGPT, n8n, AutoShorts.ai, Blotato, Postiz)

### Takeaway
Open-source generators (MoneyPrinterTurbo, MoneyPrinterV2, ShortGPT) are seen as prototypes. ## 5. "AI slop" backlash, audience reaction, and AI labeling effects on reach

### Takeaway
Backlash is now mainstream and shapes policy. Platforms give users tools to see less AI (TikTok's slider) and invest in detection (YouTube's 2026 priority, TikTok watermarks). YouTube says *labeling* AI content does not hurt reach or monetization. What gets punished is repetitiveness and inauthenticity, and evading labels (Semafor's "ghost creators") led to terminations.

### Cited Findings
- [PRESS] Kapwing: 21% of a new user's first 500 Shorts were AI and 33% brainrot; this became the basis of press coverage and YouTube's removals. — [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/one-in-five-youtube-shorts-shown-to-new-users-is-ai-generated-slop-study-finds/)
- [PRESS] NYT (via TheNextWeb): over 40% of Shorts recommended after popular preschool videos contained low-quality AI content. — [TheNextWeb](https://thenextweb.com/news/youtube-ai-slop-crackdown-faceless-creators-collateral-damage)
- [PRESS] A YouTube statement reported in coverage of the Jan 2026 removals: AI-labelled videos are not penalized in recommendations and do not lose monetization. — [Financian](https://www.financian.com/youtube-removes-major-ai-slop-channels-with-billions-of-views); [IBTimes](https://www.ibtimes.co.uk/youtube-cracks-down-ai-generated-content-1800213)
- [PRESS] TikTok's AI slider lets users reduce AI content; TikTok watermarks AI content it detects. — [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/2025/11/19/tiktok-controls-for-ai-generated-content/)
- [PRESS] Political "ghost creators" used actors to read AI scripts to avoid AI labels, and 20 channels were terminated. — [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/09/03/2026/youtube-cracks-down-on-ghost-creators)
- [HN] User sentiment: "YouTube is now full of crap AI slop" (2025) and requests to hide Shorts entirely. — [HN 45426596](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45426596); [HN 36413406](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=36413406)
- [PRESS] Human faceless creators complain of collateral damage, and some have started appearing on camera, or using a face that "might not be their own". — [Hollywood Reporter via mirror](https://hn.jakechampion.name/reader/https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/business/digital/faceless-creators-youtube-ai-damage-1236617586/); [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/youtubes-war-on-ai-slop-collides-with-faceless-creators-who-built-the-platform-first/)

### Inferences
- The agent should *always* set the YouTube altered/synthetic disclosure where it applies, and the TikTok AIGC label. Hiding AI use is now a termination-level risk with little reach upside.
- Audience-level "see less AI" controls mean AI-looking Shorts may face a shrinking pool of willing viewers over time. Distinctive style and real informational value become more important.

### Gaps
- No quantitative measurement of reach loss from AI labels on TikTok or Facebook was found. The effect of the TikTok slider on creator reach is unmeasured.
